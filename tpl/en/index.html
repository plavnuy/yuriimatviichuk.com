Monumental painting, like any work of visual art, is inseparable from the design of the interior around it. Often it is the accent that decides the style and the form the whole space will take. In a good interior everything is connected: every detail has its place and works towards one harmonious image. That is why it matters to involve the artist at the earliest stages of design, and why I prefer to carry a project through from beginning to end myself. It avoids the problems that arise when several designers see the creative task differently. The brief comes from the client — it is the client who will live or work in the result. And for that result to be good, it helps to keep to a few principles when starting something as large as a renovation, a build or the fitting-out of a home. The right team will spare you a great deal of disappointment. You can choose to be a co-author of the concept and of everything that follows, or to hand its development and execution over to us entirely, as specialists.

A few simple principles that will help you be happy with the result:

  • Decide clearly at the very start what you want to end up with. If the picture is still vague, somewhere at the edge of your mind, then show the designer concrete examples — the internet has no shortage of them — of something as close as possible to what you want.
  • Look at the market, and go only to someone whose professionalism leaves you in no doubt. Once you have chosen, trust them the way you trust your own doctor or lawyer. This matters.
  • Think and have doubts before the decision is made; afterwards, never. Doubts that come later slow the work of building down enormously.
  • Get your priorities straight. I assure you, a toilet costing a small fortune will not raise the standing of your home the way the work of a good artist will. It is expensive, but I have seen plenty of splendid interiors simply destroyed by misplaced pseudo-artistic goods.
  • An apartment or a house is, first of all, a utilitarian thing. Turn down any "prettiness" in favour of convenience without hesitation. Decor that is overdone or out of place can make a home an unpleasant place to be.
  • The easiest way to begin is with the general plan. Until every block on it is resolved, until you are sure that all the rooms are rational and convenient, do not get carried away with decor — it will find its own places later. A beautiful plan makes a beautiful home.
  • Do not forget to pay particular attention to utility rooms, where you will later hide everything that would otherwise clutter the interior. The sense of clean space in which you can move and breathe freely is what guarantees comfort and good spirits.
  • Do not begrudge the time spent studying everything your space can offer before the work starts. Many hidden possibilities in a layout only appear while the old partitions are being taken down, if you are dealing with a reconstruction — especially in older buildings. That is a convenient moment to refine the plan or change it. Time spent thinking the situation through and adjusting will help you get the most out of the space later, and will spare you unforeseen costs.

Creating an interior is a creative process, and almost always something new and unknown. I do not mean the technical side. The result of new ideas and unconventional solutions, which at first seemed brilliant and which you gladly approved, does not always please once it is built. Do not despair — go boldly on towards your ideal. It often happens that a solution which looked doubtful at first can be turned into an important and expressive element of the design. The satisfaction of knowing that everything was done impeccably, and exactly as you needed it, repays what you put into it.

I wish you nothing but pleasant feelings around the building and the fitting-out of your interior!
